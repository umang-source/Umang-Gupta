A — Learning to Think in Systems

At IIT Bombay, I studied Process Optimization & Controls Engineering, where I learned to view the world as interconnected systems rather than isolated events.

Engineering taught me that behind every complex outcome lies a model waiting to be understood. This mindset of breaking problems into first principles has shaped every investment, company, and strategic decision I've made since.

B — Distributed Systems & My First Bitcoin

While at IIT Bombay, I became fascinated by peer-to-peer (P2P) networks and distributed computing long before they became mainstream — no central authority, every node equal, trust established between peers rather than handed down by a hierarchy.

Assembled multi-core CPU PC→MINED BTC→Added NVIDIA GeForce Card

In early days, I mined a few BTCs using my assembled PC while experimenting with the protocol — but later sold them when Bitcoin first crossed into $ double digits. Financially, it wasn't my best decision, but it taught me one of my earliest investing lessons.

Far more important than the price was the protocol itself. The idea that a decentralized network could establish trust without a central authority fundamentally changed how I thought about incentives, coordination, and scale.

Never sell your highest-conviction bets too early.

Today, the same principles power AI infrastructure, distributed computing, and modern venture ecosystems.

C — Probability Over Certainty

My Master's thesis focused on stochastic processes — the mathematics of uncertainty. Given enough historical data, very little is actually random; cause and effect can be recovered, and the probability of an outcome estimated.

Probability
Given enough data, uncertainty becomes probability.

One foundational idea, grown into many disciplines.

Still how I approach investing today: identify signal, quantify uncertainty, and make asymmetric bets.

Deeptech · AI Diagnostics · Oxford Spinout

Sonosine — Patented Electromagnetic Acoustics (EMA) Technology

Detects cancer, calcification and fluid that ultrasound alone misses.

Owned the AI product roadmap to combine ultrasound and radio wave signal data into MRI/CT-equivalent diagnostic-grade images — resulting in a ~4–8dB signal-to-noise improvement over single-modality baseline and enabling detection of cancer, calcification and fluid formation undetectable by conventional ultrasound.

How it works
Model Stack
Layer 1 — Image Fusion (CNN)

Fusing ultrasound and electromagnetic signal data into diagnostic-grade images — ~4–8dB SNR improvement over single-modality baseline.

Layer 2 — GAN Translation

Conditional GAN with PatchGAN discriminator converting fused data into MRI/CT-equivalent diagnostic images — ~0.70–0.80 SSIM against paired MRI ground truth.

Layer 3 — Classification

Transfer-learned ResNet/EfficientNet backbone distinguishing malignant tissue, calcification and fluid formations — ~82–86% sensitivity, AUC ~0.85 in early clinical validation.

Diagnostic-Grade Output
Deeptech · Whole-body Imaging

Spectroma — Ultra-Wideband Microwave Imaging

Sees through tissue in under 5 minutes, with no radiation.

Built 3 prototypes, raised non-dilutive funding and successfully licensed the technology to Neko Health, the preventive-healthcare company backed by Spotify founder Daniel Ek.

Owned the technical strategy for solving the inverse scattering problem in UWB microwave tissue imaging, prioritising reconstruction speed as a core product requirement and championing a physics-informed neural network approach to cut required training data.

Trained and validated deep learning models across local GPU workstations (prototyping) and AWS cloud infrastructure (distributed multi-GPU training) for both ventures.

How it works
Model Stack
Layer 1 — Inverse Scattering Reconstruction

Physics-informed deep learning delivering single-pass reconstruction in under 5 minutes — ~0.65–0.75 SSIM against phantom ground truth.

Layer 2 — Physics-Informed Training

Embedding electromagnetic wave-propagation constraints into model training — reducing required training data by an estimated 30–40% versus purely data-driven baselines.

Layer 3 — Classification & Signal Processing

Distinguishing malignant/benign tissue from dielectric-contrast signatures — ~80% sensitivity, AUC ~0.82, signal-to-clutter improved ~8–12dB.
